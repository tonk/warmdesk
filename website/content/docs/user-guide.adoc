== Getting started

=== Registering

Open your WarmDesk URL in a browser.
If public registration is enabled you will see a *Register* link on the login page.
Fill in a username, display name, email address, and password and click *Register*.
You are logged in immediately.

If registration is not available, ask an administrator to create an account for you.

=== Logging in

Enter your username and password, or sign in with a registered *passkey*.
WarmDesk issues a short-lived JWT access token (15 minutes) and a 7-day refresh token that keeps you logged in as long as your browser tab is open.

By default the session expires after 60 minutes of inactivity; your administrator can change or disable this timeout.

=== Forgotten password

Click *Forgot password?* on the login page and enter your email address.
If an account with that address exists, a reset link is sent immediately.
The link is valid for one hour.

=== Multi-factor authentication (MFA)

You can enable TOTP-based MFA from *Settings → Security*.
Scan the QR code with an authenticator app (Aegis, Authy, Google Authenticator, etc.) and enter the 6-digit code to confirm.
Once enabled, every login requires the one-time code after the password.
If your administrator allows it, you can mark a device as trusted to skip MFA on it for 7 or 30 days.

=== Desktop app

WarmDesk is also available as a native desktop app (Linux AppImage, macOS DMG, Windows installer) that asks for your server URL on first run.

* *Profiles* — connect to several WarmDesk servers with fully separate sessions and settings (`warmdesk --create-profile customer-a`, then `warmdesk --profile customer-a`).
* *System tray* — an optional tray icon shows an unread badge; closing the window can minimise it to the tray.
When connected to a test server, the tray icon turns orange and gets a "(TEST)" suffix.

== Interface overview

The collapsible, resizable sidebar (left or right) contains drag-to-reorder sections:

* *Favorite Projects* / *Projects* — your starred projects and all projects you belong to
* *Favorite Customers* / *All Customers* — customers you have been assigned to
* *Favorite People* / *People* — online users; click a name to open a direct message
* *Chats* — your most recent conversations with unread indicators
* *Tickets* — your helpdesk inbox and per-customer ticket lists (helpdesk users only)

Use the *⋮* menu at the top of the sidebar to show or hide whole sections you never use.

The header holds the global search bar, quick links to *Dashboard*, *News*, *Chats* and *Time Tracking*, the language and theme switchers, a *?* button for contextual help on the current page, and your avatar menu (settings, admin panel, *Keyboard Shortcuts*, guide PDFs under *Downloads*, *About*, *Logout*).
Press `?` anywhere to see all keyboard shortcuts.

The *notification bell* in the top bar shows unread @mentions and card updates.

== Projects

=== Creating a project

Click *+ New Project* on the dashboard.
Choose a name, a *card prefix* (e.g. `PRJ`, used in card numbers and fixed after creation), a colour, and a board type (Kanban or Scrum).

If you don't see the *+ New Project* button, your administrator has not granted you permission to create projects.

=== Board types

*Kanban* — columns you define, cards flow freely between columns.
Suitable for continuous-flow work.

*Scrum* — same column structure but with sprints, a backlog, epics, and story points.

=== Members and roles

Open *Project Settings → Members* to invite people as *Viewer* (read-only), *Member* (create and edit cards, comment, chat), *Admin* (also manage columns), or *Owner* (also manage members and webhooks, delete the project).

=== Starring and ordering

Star a project with the ⭐ icon to pin it to *Favorite Projects* in the sidebar.
Drag favourites to reorder them.
Filter the dashboard by customer using the dropdown at the top.

=== Closing a project

Owners can close a project from *Project Settings → General → Danger Zone*.
A closed project is hidden from the sidebar and project list but stays reachable by its URL and can be reopened at any time.

== Kanban board

=== Columns

Add columns with *+ Add Column* in the board toolbar (project admins and owners).
Click a column title to rename it; drag the column header to reorder; remove an empty column with the trash icon.

Open *Column settings* (⚙) on a column header to change its colour and optional *WIP limit* (maximum cards).
The header shows count versus limit (for example `3 / 5`); it is emphasized when the column is at or over the limit.
Leave the limit empty to turn off WIP tracking.

=== Cards

Click *+ Add card* at the bottom of a column to create a new card.
Drag cards between columns and within a column to reorder them, or use a column's sort controls (date, assignee, priority).
Click a card to open the card detail panel.

=== Table view

The *Table View* button in the board toolbar swaps the lanes for a flat, sortable table of every card in the project: reference, title, column, priority, assignee, due date, story points, time spent, and closed state.
Click a column header to sort (click again to reverse), and a title to open the card.
*Hide Closed* works in both views, and your choice of view is remembered in this browser.

=== Gantt chart

Click *Gantt* in the board toolbar for a timeline of every card with a start or due date.
Switch between Day, Week, and Month zoom; click a bar to open the card.

=== Scrum extras

Scrum projects add a *Backlog* view (prioritise cards by dragging, assign them to sprints), an *Epics* view for grouping cards across sprints, and a *Sprint Board* showing only the active sprint.
Create a sprint, add backlog cards to it, and click *Start* to begin.
When you complete a sprint, unfinished cards return to the backlog.

=== Charts

Click *📊 Charts* in the board toolbar to open the analytics view.
Kanban projects include four charts:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Chart |What it shows

|*CFD* (Cumulative Flow Diagram)
|Daily card counts per column over the selected period.
A widening band for a column signals growing WIP or a blocked stage.

|*Cycle Time*
|Control chart of how long each card spent open, plotted by close date, with a 7-day rolling average.

|*Lead Time*
|Distribution of total time from card creation to closure, including any waiting time in the initial column.

|*Throughput*
|Number of cards closed per week over the selected period — a direct measure of delivery rate.
|===

Use the time-range selectors at the top of each chart to adjust the window.

image::../../images/screenshots/14-cumulative.png[Cumulative flow diagram, 900]

Scrum projects add these charts:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Chart |What it shows

|*Velocity*
|Committed versus completed story points per sprint — helps forecast future sprint capacity.

|*Burndown*
|Remaining story points versus the ideal burn line; shows whether the sprint is on track.

|*Burnup*
|Work completed versus total scope — useful for visualising scope creep mid-sprint.

|*Sprint Report*
|Completed and not-completed cards for a finished sprint, with a completion percentage.

|*Epic Burndown*
|Remaining cards and points for a single epic over time.

|*Release Burndown*
|Progress across all sprints in a release; shows remaining scope toward the release goal.
|===

== Cards

A card is the basic unit of work.
Open any card to access its full set of fields.

=== Basic fields

* *Title* — required, short description of the work (Markdown formatting is rendered)
* *Description* — Markdown with toolbar, emoji picker, and @mention autocomplete
* *Priority* — none / low / medium / high / critical (colour-coded)
* *Due date* / *Start date*
* *Assignees* — one or more team members; their avatars appear on the board
* *Labels* — colour-coded labels defined per project (manage in Project Settings); *Tags* are free-form hashtags
* *Story points* — for Scrum velocity tracking (when enabled by the administrator)
* *Epic* — Scrum projects only

Use the *⋮* menu in the card's top-right corner to show or hide optional sections (attachments, checklist, labels, tags, watchers, sub-cards, linked cards, …); a section with content is always shown.

=== Checklist

Add checklist items in the *Checklist* section and drag the ⠿ handle to reorder them.
Each item has its own checkbox; the card shows a completion ratio (e.g. `3/5`).

=== Comments and time logging

Add a comment at the bottom of the card detail; click *Reply* under a comment to quote it.
Comments can carry attachments too — paste a screenshot or attach a file directly in the comment box.
To log time with the comment, fill in the hours and minutes fields.
Logged time appears in the board time report.

=== File attachments

Drag files or click the upload zone to attach them.
Images are displayed inline; other file types appear as download links.
MIME type is detected server-side; the client-supplied content type is ignored.

=== Relationships

* *Sub-cards* — create child cards for hierarchical decomposition
* *Cross-references* — link any two cards across projects with a _relates-to_ badge
* *Git links* — commits, pull requests, and issues from GitHub, GitLab, Gitea, or Forgejo (added automatically via webhooks or manually)

=== Copy, transfer, and close

* *Copy Card* duplicates the card in the same column.
* *Transfer* copies or moves the card to another project. A move keeps the comments, checklist, attachments, and history; the card gets a new number there, and its old number keeps working. Labels are matched by name, the epic and sprint are cleared, assignees without access to the target project are removed, and sub-cards can move along or stay behind.
* *Close Card* marks it done (it stays visible, struck through); *Reopen Card* restores it.

=== Watchers

Add yourself or colleagues as watchers to receive notifications when the card is updated.

=== Card numbers

Every card gets a sequential number like `PRJ-42`.
Type this number in any chat message or comment to generate a clickable badge that opens the card, or use it in commit messages to link git events to the card.
A card moved to another project gets a new number there, but its old number keeps working everywhere.

== Topics (threaded discussions)

Each project has a *Topics* page alongside the Kanban board.
Topics are threaded discussion threads — good for design decisions, meeting notes, and async conversations that don't map to a single card.

* *Pin* a topic to keep it at the top
* *Reply* to any topic to add a response
* *Edit* or *delete* your own topics and replies

== Team chat

=== Project chat

Every project has a persistent chat channel, opened from the *Project Chat* entry in the sidebar of the project's *Topics* page.
All project members can read and post, including pasted screenshots and file attachments.
Use the *⋮* menu at the top of the Topics sidebar to hide the entry if you don't use it.

=== Direct messages

Open *Chats* in the header (or click a name under *People* in the sidebar) to access direct messages and group chats.

Start a 1-on-1 conversation from any user, or click *New Conversation* and create a *group chat* by selecting multiple users.
The *Teams* tab pre-fills all members of one of your projects in a single click.
Group chats support a custom avatar and member management (add / remove participants).

Chat messages support:

* *Markdown* formatting, including syntax-highlighted code blocks
* *File attachments* — images shown inline, other files as links; paste an image from the clipboard to attach it
* *Emoji reactions* — react to any message; hover to see who reacted
* *@mentions* — triggers a notification and highlights the name in the recipient's view
* *Link previews* — Open Graph metadata is fetched server-side
* *Layout options* — bubble, comfortable, compact, cozy, or grouped (Discord-style)
* *Desktop notifications* — enabled via the bell icon when your browser prompts; while the tab is in the background its title also blinks with the sender's name

=== Voice and video calls

WarmDesk supports two call modes in direct messages:

* *1:1 audio/video* in one-on-one conversations (WebRTC peer-to-peer)
* *Group calls* in any group conversation (LiveKit)

Use the camera button in the chat header to start a video call, or the mic button next to it for a voice-only call that never asks for camera access.
During a call you can share your screen, see when the other party is muted, and use a resizable in-call text chat.
If the server has not configured LiveKit yet, group chats show a status banner explaining that group calls are unavailable.

Click the pop-out button in the call controls to shrink the call into a small floating window that you can move and resize (also with the keyboard), so you can keep working while on the call; click it again to return to full screen.

==== Inviting people to an active call

While in any call, click the *+* button (add person icon) in the bottom controls bar to invite additional participants.
A user picker lets you search by name and select one or more people.
Each selected user receives a real-time popup with a *Join* button.

If you are in a *1:1 call*, inviting someone upgrades the call automatically to a LiveKit group room — your existing call partner also receives the join popup.
Users who are offline or decline simply do not appear in the room.

== Notifications and @mentions

The notification bell in the top bar shows unread items.
Click it to open the notification panel.

You receive a notification when:

* Someone @mentions you in a card comment, topic reply, or chat message
* A card you are watching is updated
* A card assigned to you is moved or commented on

If you are online, a mention also shows a popup in the corner of the screen; if you are offline, WarmDesk emails you (when SMTP is configured).

== Time tracking

Time tracking is an optional module enabled per user.
The *Time Tracking* page (`/time-tracking`) has these tabs:

* *Log Time* — the weekly grid or the calendar view
* *Report* — your personal time report with PDF/XLSX export
* *Board* — time logged on project cards (for users with report access)
* *Invoices* — invoices across your customers (when invoicing is available to you)

Time-tracking viewers and administrators get an *Employee* dropdown to view another user's sheet or report.

=== Weekly grid

Each row is a customer + project + activity combination; columns are the days of the week, with today highlighted.
Click a cell to enter the hours worked that day; row, day, and week totals update automatically.

* Hover a cell for buttons to set a *time range* (⏱), a travel *distance* (⇆), or mark it as a *holiday*.
* Link a row to a *contract* to show its rate and, if the contract has time slots, fill the week from them.
* Edit a row's customer, project, contract, location, or activity with ✎ or by double-clicking it.
* *Copy Previous Week* copies the row layout without hours; *Undo* reverts the last change.
* Export the week as XLSX or PDF, or a printable *Grid PDF* for a whole week, month, or year.

=== Copy, cut, and paste

Select a cell, then *Shift+click* or *Shift+arrow keys* to extend a rectangular selection.
`Ctrl+C` / `Ctrl+X` / `Ctrl+V` (`⌘` on macOS) copy, cut, and paste minutes, start/end times, holiday flag, and distance.
A single copied value fills every selected cell; a copied block pastes as a grid.
The clipboard survives switching weeks, so you can copy Monday of one week into Monday of another.

=== Calendar view

Click the 📅 button next to *Edit macros* to switch the Log Time tab between the weekly grid and a drag-and-drop calendar (choose which one opens by default in Settings).
Entries with a start and end time appear as coloured blocks; entries without times are listed in an *Unscheduled* strip.
Blocks are coloured by customer by default, or by project via the *Calendar block color* setting.

* *Click* an empty slot to create a one-hour entry, or *click and drag* to create one for the exact range.
* *Drag* a block to move it to another time or day; drag its top or bottom edge to *resize* it.
* *Right-click* a block to *Edit*, *Copy*, or *Delete* it; right-click an empty slot to create an entry or *Paste* the copied one (same customer, project, activity, distance, and location, and the same duration).
* Use *−* / *+* in the top-right corner to *zoom*; the zoom level is remembered in your browser.
* Tab to a block and press `Enter` to edit its date and times with the keyboard.

Macros, *Copy Previous Week*, *Undo*, and the export buttons work in the calendar view too.

[[travel-distance-auto-fill]]
=== Travel distance auto-fill

If a customer has <<customers,locations>> with a standard travel distance, a *Location* picker appears wherever a distance can be set: the add/edit row forms, the per-cell distance popup, the calendar entry form, and macro rows.
Picking a location fills in its distance (still editable afterwards) and remembers the chosen location on the entry.
Re-picking a row's location also updates that week's entries on the row that already have a distance; days left blank stay untouched.

=== Time macros

Store recurring weekly patterns (for example travel out, preparation, teaching, travel home) as *time macros*.
Open the editor with *⚡* in the top bar to create, duplicate, import, or export macros; each row presets customer, project, activity, hours, optional start/end times, distance, and location — either the same for every day or as an alternating A/B pattern.
Run a macro from *⚡ Macro* in the bottom bar: pick the macro and the start day, and it fills the current week.
Macros are stored on the server, so they follow you across devices.

=== Time-tracking-only projects and customers

The ⚙ button on the time-tracking tab bar manages lightweight projects and customers that exist only for time tracking (no board, no CRM entry).
A project can have *undeclarable minutes* (e.g. travel time) that are subtracted from declarable totals in the sheet, report, and exports.
Projects and customers created by an administrator are marked *Global (created by admin)* and are visible to everyone.

=== Timer from the command line

`warmdesk-timer` starts a timer when you begin and books the time when you stop.
The Linux desktop packages (`.deb`, `.rpm`) install it as `/usr/bin/warmdesk-timer`; it also comes with the server download and as a single file for Linux, macOS, and Windows on the release page.
Give it your server and a personal API key (*Settings → API Keys*) through `WARMDESK_URL` / `WARMDESK_API_KEY` or a `timer.yaml` config file with `url:` and `api_key:`.

[source,bash]
----
warmdesk-timer start Website -m "homepage"   # or: start Travel -c Acme
warmdesk-timer status
warmdesk-timer stop                          # stop --at 17:30 if you forgot
----

Starting while a timer runs books the running one first.
Time is rounded up to whole 15 minutes and split at midnight; the entries show up in the weekly grid, calendar, and reports like any other.
Run `warmdesk-timer projects` to see what you can book on, and `warmdesk-timer help` for all options.

=== Reports

The *Report* tab shows your own entries for a week, month, year, or custom range, grouped by period, customer, project, or customer + project.
Switch between a table and a chart (bar, pie, or stacked bar).
*Export options* control font, PDF language, a page per customer, costs, distances, and undeclarable time before you download a *PDF* or *XLSX*.
When grouped by customer, billable groups offer a *Create Invoice* button.

The *Board* tab (formerly *Reports*, which still redirects there) reports time logged on cards per project, filterable by period, project, and assignees, and exports to PDF or XLSX.
All exports are generated on the server.

== Helpdesk

When the helpdesk is enabled for you, a *Tickets* section appears in the sidebar with an *Inbox* (email tickets not yet assigned to a customer) and one ticket list per customer.

* Tickets move through *New*, *Open*, *Pending* (with an optional reminder date), *Pending close* (closed automatically on its close date), and *Closed*.
* In a ticket you can change status, priority, type, assignee, group, and tags, see SLA deadlines, and link other tickets or board cards.
* Reply to the customer, or tick *Private* to post an internal note that is never emailed and hidden from customer-role users.
* Apply a *macro* to set several fields and pre-fill a reply in one click, or a checklist template; open checklist items block closing the ticket.
* *Mark as Spam* closes a ticket and hides it from the default list.

Users with the *customer* role only see the helpdesk, and only their own customers' tickets.

== Customers

If your organisation uses customer management:

* You only see customers you have been assigned to (administrators see all)
* Star a customer for quick access
* The customer detail page shows its projects, contracts (with rates and optional time slots), billing information, contacts, and invoices
* *Locations* list the customer's sites, each with a name, address, contact details, and a standard *travel distance* used by <<travel-distance-auto-fill,travel distance auto-fill>>
* Give a customer a *colour* to identify its blocks in the time-tracking calendar; customers without one get an unused colour automatically

Customer admins can edit the customer, manage its contracts, contacts, and locations, and add or remove members.

== Search

The global search bar (shortcut: `Ctrl+K`, `⌘K` on macOS) searches across:

* Card titles, descriptions, and comments
* Project chat and direct messages (in conversations you belong to)
* Ticket subjects (with helpdesk access)
* Time-entry descriptions

Queries need at least 3 characters.
Search results are scoped to your accessible resources, and clicking a comment or time entry jumps straight to it.

=== Search & replace

Click the ⇄ icon next to the search box to find and replace text across Cards, Card comments, Direct messages, Tickets, and Time entries.
Choose the categories and how many results per type to show, then click *Preview* to see a before/after snippet for every match.
Matches you are not allowed to edit are greyed out; uncheck anything you want to keep and click *Replace* to update only the checked rows.
Project chat messages can be searched but not replaced.
